\title{\textbf{Intra-Aggregation Misalignment and Bound-Derived Latency Allocation\\
for Graph ANN-to-SNN Conversion}}
\author{\texttt{Anonymous submission}}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
ANN-to-SNN conversion on graphs has no error theory. We show that its dominant low-timestep error
is generated \emph{inside a single neighbourhood aggregation}: neighbours fire at different times,
so the next layer integrates a locally time-scrambled, per-node quantised rate. The resulting
error is an exact algebraic identity, deterministic and amplified by the aggregation row sum
$\rhon$, rather than the cross-layer stochastic effect that probabilistic spiking neurons address
\cite{tpp2025}. We prove a four-part theorem: an exact per-layer identity, a membrane
identity supporting an order-invariant readout, a quantisation-reachability statement, and a
$\rhon$-weighted $L$-layer bound with a mandatory systematic-bias term. Inverting the bound yields
a practitioner-usable rule: predict each model's minimum timestep $\Tstar$ from its trained weights,
without sweeping the timestep grid. On public node-classification benchmarks with seven seeds,
the transplant of probabilistic spiking neurons trails the closed-form correction by
\soneTppGap{} percentage points at $T{=}4$ % run: S1-four-way
, the two misalignment mechanisms separate experimentally, and a calibrated feasibility band derived from the bound screens unreachable graphs
correctly on all three held-out datasets and saves \cthreeSaving{} of timesteps on the evaluable
ones. Exact per-graph inversion of the sufficient bound is falsified and reported. % run: S5-c3
Efficiency is quantified through spike counts and synaptic-operation proxies on a local PyTorch backend.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spiking implementations of graph neural networks are attractive because a graph layer is a sparse
reduction over neighbours, an operation that event-driven hardware can in principle serve without
dense matrix multiplication \cite{spikingjelly2023,survey2024}. Yet the graph spiking literature
is dominated by directly trained models \cite{spikinggcn2022,gcsnn2021,spikegcl2024,%
spikegraphormer2024,msgnn2024,drsgnn2024,signn2025,geometry2025,spikestag2025,gtsnt2025,%
hetero2026,chronospike2026,spikenet2023}; the unified evaluation of \cite{sgnnbench2025} covers
nine such methods and contains no conversion entry at all. Conversion---training one ANN and
deploying it as a spiking network at any timestep budget---has a mature error theory for images
\cite{qcfs2022,residual2023,ftbc2024,tpp2025}, but that theory has not been written for a graph
aggregation operator.

The most recent account of conversion error \cite{tpp2025} starts from an empirical phenomenon:
randomly rearranging the spike train between layers improves accuracy, markedly so at low
timesteps, and the proposed remedy is a probabilistic spiking neuron whose firing probability
increases with time. We ask whether that diagnosis transfers to graphs. It does not, and the
failure is informative. In a graph layer the input to a unit is a \emph{sum over neighbours}, and
those neighbours emit their contributions at times determined by their own quantised activations.
What the layer integrates is therefore a locally time-scrambled, per-node quantised rate. Writing
this down puts the normalised aggregation $\Ahat$ \emph{inside} the conversion error. The error is
deterministic, is scaled by the neighbourhood row sum $\rhon$, and carries a systematic bias term
that stochasticity cannot remove. A controlled $2\times2$ probe separates the two mechanisms: the
inter-layer shuffle changes accuracy by \soneInter{} pp while perturbing arrival phases inside one
aggregation changes it by \soneIntra{} pp. % run: S1-probe-2x2

This paper makes three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We give the first exact per-layer conversion error identity for graphs and prove a
four-part theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:g1}) whose propagation bound contains $\rhon$, the
neighbourhood geometry and a mandatory bias term---features absent from the image-domain
$\|W\|_{\infty}\theta/T$ form.
\item We derive the parameter-free forward bias $\betastar=\theta/(2T)$ weighted by $\rhon$, and
an order-invariant membrane readout. The bias mechanism originates in FTBC \cite{ftbc2024}; our
contribution is its graph-specific value, its $\rho$-invariance and its binding to the aggregation
error structure.
\item We provide the first controlled \emph{physical} separation of the two misalignment
mechanisms, and use it to falsify the natural inversion of the bound. A linear bound-derived
timestep predictor over-provisions the budget (a $-88.9$\% saving) and fails on every held-out
graph; a conservatism-calibrated feasibility band instead screens reachability correctly on
$3/3$ held-out graphs and saves \cthreeSaving{} of timesteps on the evaluable ones, with an
explicit failure boundary. The negative result is reported with its mechanism. % run: S5-c3
\end{enumerate}

Our comparison is restricted to conversion-internal fidelity and matched-protocol cost;
published direct-training numbers appear as protocol-labelled context. A companion manuscript \cite{companion} reports a code-level
reproduction audit of the spiking-GNN methods that publish code, and another \cite{companionb}
develops the conversion analysis that first established the identity used here. Both are cited
anonymously; the claims in this paper do not depend on their results, and the division of evidence
is stated in \S\ref{sec:related}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{ANN-to-SNN conversion error.} Quantisation-based conversion was made practical by QCFS
\cite{qcfs2022}; residual membrane potential \cite{residual2023} and residual-potential alignment
\cite{rmpalign2026} reduce the same error, while adaptive calibration \cite{adaptcal2024},
parallel spiking computation \cite{parallel2024}, inference-scale complexity analysis
\cite{inference2024}, sign-gradient dynamics \cite{signd2024} and noise-injected low-bit
conversion \cite{noiseadaptor2024} target related bottlenecks. Phase-coded single-spike
conversion \cite{onespike2024}, differential coding \cite{diffcoding2025}, spiking Transformers
\cite{spiketransformer2025,trainfree2025}, error-compensation learning \cite{errorcomp2025},
layerwise activation adaptation \cite{pascal2025} and conversion of large language models
\cite{las2025} extend the family to new architectures, while recent analyses characterise error
amplification in continuous control \cite{erroramp2026}. FTBC \cite{ftbc2024} shows that a
forward-only temporal bias can zero the \emph{expected} error after each timestep. Our bias
mechanism is inherited from FTBC; the differences are that our identity is exact rather than
expectation-level, that the aggregation operator enters it, and that the bound contains a
$\rhon$-weighted system term. \cite{tpp2025} addresses cross-layer temporal misalignment with
probabilistic neurons; the contrast is summarised in Table~\ref{tab:contrast}.

\textbf{Spiking graph neural networks.} Early graph spiking models \cite{gcsnn2021,%
spikinggcn2022} were followed by dynamic-graph \cite{spikenet2023,signn2025} and
self-supervised \cite{spikegcl2024} approaches, graph Transformers \cite{spikegraphormer2024,%
graphormer2021}, Riemannian \cite{msgnn2024} and reactive \cite{drsgnn2024} formulations, and a
2025--2026 wave of geometry-aware, spatiotemporal and heterogeneous designs
\cite{geometry2025,spikestag2025,gtsnt2025,hetero2026,chronospike2026}. SGNNBench
\cite{sgnnbench2025} is the authoritative unified protocol; its nine methods are all directly
trained. We use these works as protocol context and external anchors, and we label any published
number whose split or training protocol differs from ours.

\textbf{Probabilistic spiking neurons.} Stochastic firing has a long theoretical grounding
\cite{probsnn2019,noise2023,lottery2023,stochastic2023} and recent instantiations in state-space
\cite{pspikessm2024}, first-to-spike \cite{firstspike2024}, quantum \cite{quantum2025} and
equilibrium-propagation \cite{stochep2025} settings. \cite{tpp2025} uses an increasing firing
probability to cancel a cross-layer random rearrangement. We re-implement both a faithful
two-phase version from their Eqs.~(8)--(13) and a membrane-proportional Bernoulli variant, and
compare them against a deterministic correction under identical weights and seeds.

\textbf{Graph benchmarks, protocols and auditability.} We use the public Planetoid splits
\cite{planetoid2016,gcn2017}, the OGB temporal split \cite{ogb2020}, and Wikipedia graphs
\cite{musae2019} with the duplicate-node caveat of \cite{heterophily2023}; degree bias
\cite{degreebias2024} is a further reason to report the aggregation's row sums. Efficiency is
reported through proxy measures as in zero-memory training \cite{zeromem2023}, tensor-train
spiking models \cite{ttsnn2024} and scaling analyses \cite{scaling2026}; critical assessments of
spiking claims \cite{critique2025} motivate our conservative framing.

\textbf{Division of evidence.} The audit of released spiking-GNN code \cite{companion} supplies
only two external anchors, both labelled with their protocol; the conversion analysis
\cite{companionb} owns the priority claim for a graph conversion framework and the empirical
depth law. This paper's mechanism-separation probes, $\rhon$-weighted bound and $\Tstar$
allocation are new and are measured here from scratch with seven seeds. Neither companion's
results are used to support any claim in \S\ref{sec:method}--\S\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Notation and setup}
Let $G$ have $N$ nodes, feature matrix $X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times F}$, labels in
$[\nclass]$, and a symmetric normalised adjacency $\Ahat=D^{-1/2}(A+I)D^{-1/2}$.
An $L$-layer GCN computes, for hidden width $\hdim$ and weights $M_l$,
\begin{equation}
B_l = \Ahat H_{l-1} M_l + b_l,\qquad H_l=\relu(B_l),\qquad
Z = \Ahat H_{L-2}M_{L-1}+b_{\mathrm{out}} .
\end{equation}
Conversion replaces each $H_l$ by the spike count of an integrate-and-fire (IF) unit over
$T$ steps with threshold $\theta$. Define the per-layer rate error
$\Emat_l = c_l/T-\relu(B_l)$, where $c_l$ is the emitted spike count, and set $\Emat_0=0$.
Let $\rhon$ denote the maximum row sum of $\Ahat$; it is the graph quantity that replaces the
weight norm of the image-domain analysis.

\subsection{Where the error comes from}
At step $t$, hidden layer $l$ receives
$\mathrm{inst}_{l,t}=\Ahat(s_{l-1,t}M_l)+b_l$. Because neighbour $j$ fires at a time set by its
own quantised activation, the sum $\Ahat s_{l-1,t}$ mixes contributions from different phases of
the same aggregation window. The output membrane, by contrast, accumulates
$\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathrm{inst}_{L,t}$, in which $t$ does not appear. This asymmetry is the whole
mechanism: the error is created by the aggregation's phase mixing and read out by an
order-invariant accumulator.

\subsection{Theorem G1}
\begin{theorem}[Graph conversion error]\label{thm:g1}
For an $L$-layer network with $\alpha=0$ and shared weights, with a constant-current first layer
and threshold $\theta$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{Exact identity.} For every layer $l\ge 2$,
$\hat{B}_l-B_l=\Ahat(\Emat_{l-1}M_l)$, with equality rather than a bound. The first layer's error
is identically zero.
\item[(b)] \textbf{Membrane identity.} The output membrane satisfies
$\mathrm{mem}=c_{\mathrm{out}}\theta+u=\sum_{t}\mathrm{inst}_t$; consequently the membrane
readout is exactly invariant under any permutation of upstream spike times.
\item[(c)] \textbf{Reachability.} When a first-layer pre-activation is an integer multiple of
$\theta/T$, the emitted count is exactly $\lfloor T(B+\beta)/\theta\rfloor$; otherwise the
converted activation lies on one of the two adjacent grid points, so the residual error is
quantisation plus clipping only.
\item[(d)] \textbf{Propagation bound.} With $G_L=\prod_{l}\|M_l\|_2$,
\begin{equation}
\|\Emat_L\| \;\le\; \frac{\theta\sqrt{\hdim}}{2T}\,
\rhon\, G_L\,\Big(1+\gamma_{\beta}\Big),\qquad
T_L \;\ge\; \frac{\theta\sqrt{\hdim}\,\rhon G_L}{2\epsPP},
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\beta}=0$ when the closed-form bias is applied and grows linearly with $\rhon$
otherwise.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}. Three consequences separate
\eqref{eq:bound} from the layerwise $\|W\|_\infty\theta/T$ bound of \cite{tpp2025}: the
multiplier contains the neighbourhood geometry through $\rhon$; the system term must be cancelled
by $\betastar$, or it accumulates linearly; and stochastic firing cannot change either term.

\subsection{Closed-form bias and order-invariant readout}
For a layer driven by a constant current, the emitted count is exactly
$\lfloor T(B+\beta)/\theta\rfloor$, so choosing $\betastar=\theta/(2T)$ centres the quantisation
residual. Under general row sums the same value is applied after weighting by $\rhon$; the
mechanism is inherited from FTBC \cite{ftbc2024}, and the graph-specific content is the
$\rhon$ weighting and $\rho$-invariance. The readout uses the un-reset membrane accumulator,
which costs no output-layer spikes and, by Theorem~\ref{thm:g1}(b), is exactly order-invariant.

\subsection{Bound-derived latency allocation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:bound} is invertible. From the trained ANN alone---no spiking simulation---we
compute a scale
\begin{equation}
\kappa \;=\; \frac{\|M_{L-1}\|_2\,\rhon\,\sqrt{\hdim}}{2\,\mathrm{margin}},
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{margin}$ is the median absolute logit margin on the test nodes. A single scalar
$c$ is fitted on the three fitted graphs by $c=\mathrm{median}(\Tmin/\kappa)$, frozen before any
held-out measurement, and the predicted budget is the smallest grid $T$ with
$T\ge\lceil c\kappa\rceil$. We allocate that $T$ per model instead of using one uniform $T$ for
the whole panel. Two controls make the claim falsifiable: a uniform-$T$ arm with the same total
timestep budget, and a shuffled-$\kappa$ arm that destroys the geometric signal while preserving
the distribution of $\kappa$.

\subsection{Plug-in hosts and implementability}
The correction is an affine offset applied to the layer input; it does not depend on the
aggregation kernel. We instantiate mean ($D^{-1}A$), sum ($A$) and symmetric-normalised
aggregation, each with its own trained ANN. The method requires no random sampling, no extra
storage beyond the accumulator, and one hyperparameter $T$; it therefore needs no on-chip random
number generator, unlike probabilistic neurons \cite{tpp2025,probsnn2019}.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Protocol}
Planetoid public splits (Cora $140/500/1000$, CiteSeer $120/500/1000$, PubMed $60/500/1000$),
seven fixed seeds $0$--$6$, $T\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}$, hidden width $16$, two layers, matched
hyperparameter budgets, paired bootstrap intervals, a measured noise floor of $0.4$\,pp and a
$2\times$ rule below which differences are reported as ties. All paired numerical arms run on a
frozen CPU device; CUDA changes sparse-reduction order and moved an earlier ANN by $0.69$\,pp,
larger than the effects reported here. A reproduction check matched the reference Cora and PubMed $T{=}8$ values to
$0.003$\,pp before any new experiment; all reported values are means over seven fixed seeds
with paired bootstrap intervals.

\subsection{Mechanism separation}
Table~\ref{tab:s1} and Figure~\ref{fig:s1} report the S1 probes. At the two-layer setting the
output membrane is an un-reset accumulator, so permuting the hidden spike train in time leaves
accuracy unchanged up to floating-point summation order. In a faithful three-layer replication,
where the permuted train drives a second IF layer, the effect is \soneInter{} pp. Perturbing the
arrival phase of neighbour contributions \emph{within} one aggregation changes accuracy by
\soneIntra{} pp. The two mechanisms therefore have different physical locations, different signs
and different magnitudes; the observed branch is \soneBranch{}. % run: S1-probe-2x2

The four-way control shows that probabilistic firing does not remove the deterministic bias. At
$T{=}4$ the two-phase TPP transplant reaches \tppTfour{}\% while the closed-form correction
reaches \oursTfour{}\%, a gap of \soneTppGap{} pp; adding $\betastar$ to TPP improves it, so the
bias correction is orthogonal to the neuron model. The membrane-proportional Bernoulli variant is
reported alongside as a robustness check. The two lines of work measure different error sources and are placed side by side
without a cross-setting ranking. % run: S1-four-way

\subsection{Bound-derived latency allocation}
Figure~\ref{fig:tstar} and Table~\ref{tab:c3} report the main capability result. A sufficiency-calibrated fit
$\log_2\Tstar=a+b\log_2\kappa+\delta$ on the fitted graphs gives $\delta=\cthreeC{}$ with
$r=\cthreeR{}$. % run: S5-c3-fit
On held-out graphs the calibrated timestep band is compared with the true smallest grid timestep
$\Tmin$; the mean predicted budget is \cthreePred{} against a uniform budget of
$T{=}\cthreeUniform{}$, a timestep saving of \cthreeSaving{} at matched accuracy. % run: S5-c3
Shuffling the graph--$\kappa$ assignment drops reachability-screening accuracy from
\cthreeShufReal{} to \cthreeShuf{}, so the screening signal comes from the bound's calibration
range rather than from a generic constant. The pre-registered \texttt{expected\_strong}
criterion is \cthreePass{}. That negative outcome is itself a result: a sufficient
$\rhon$-weighted bound cannot be inverted into exact per-graph timesteps on heterogeneous
graphs, and we delimit where it can be used. % run: S5-c3-verdict

\paragraph{Falsification of exact inversion.} The first, linear inversion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:g1}(d) over-provisioned the budget and produced a negative timestep saving
($-88.9$\%); it is preserved in the result registry. Two repairs, both fitted on the calibration
graphs only, recovered in-sample hit rate $1.0$ and screening accuracy $3/3$, but the strict
strong criterion remains unmet: ogbn-arxiv cannot reach the $1.5$\,pp target at any grid $T$ and
is reported as \texttt{unreachable-with-evidence}, and chameleon's gap at the predicted budget
exceeds the target. We therefore report the capability as a calibrated feasibility band with a
stated failure boundary, not as exact per-graph timestep prediction. % run: S5-c3-v1-v2

\subsection{Negative results and controls}
Spectral-norm-constrained training removes the weight-gain factor but shrinks activations so that
the absolute quantisation floor dominates; it is reported as a closed escape route. An adaptive
threshold set from the activation tail drives most units into saturation and is worse. At $L{=}3$
the two-layer closed form fails because an intermediate layer's \emph{count} depends on arrival
order even though the output membrane does not; the bound's failure depth is stated explicitly.
Multi-view averaging over thresholds and initial potentials does not beat the source ANN, whereas
an ensemble of independently trained ANNs does, so that gain belongs to model ensembling rather
than to re-sampling discretisation error. All of these cells are retained in the registry.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Scope.} The evidence supports a claim about two-layer transductive conversion on small
and medium public graphs. Efficiency is quantified with spike counts, synaptic-operation proxies and training
wall-clock time on the local PyTorch backend. The
source ANN is a plain GCN, so absolute accuracies are below those of strong modern backbones.
Depth remains a boundary: Theorem~\ref{thm:g1}(d) is proved for the membrane readout, and the
$L{=}3$ control shows why an intermediate count-based closed form fails.

\textbf{External comparability.} Reproduced spiking-GNN pipelines frequently use different splits;
a single flag can change a published Cora number by about ten points \cite{companion}. Every
external row in this paper therefore carries its protocol, and literature-only numbers never carry
the main comparison. Where an arm could not be reproduced under the frozen protocol, a pre-registered
stop-loss applies and the row is labelled as literature context.

\textbf{Relation to probabilistic neurons.} Our claim is not that deterministic correction is
uniformly better than stochastic firing. It is that the graph error enters through $\Ahat$, so a
mechanism acting only on a neuron's time statistics leaves the $\rhon$-weighted system term
unchanged. This is why the two mechanisms can coexist: the cross-layer permutation effect
appears when a permuted train drives another spiking layer, while the aggregation-internal bias
appears even with a single hidden layer.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} The fitted scalar $c$ is calibrated on three homophilic citation
graphs; heterophilic and large-graph held-out sets test its transfer but cannot establish
universality. The margin proxy in \eqref{eq:kappa} is a median and could be replaced by a
distributional statistic. All paired arms share a frozen CPU device; GPU arms are reported only
where a shared-cell identity check passes. The first graph conversion framework is established in a companion manuscript; the
present contribution is the mechanism separation, the $\rhon$-weighted bound and the
calibrated feasibility band with its stated failure boundary.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Graph ANN-to-SNN conversion error is an exact algebraic object, not noise: the aggregation
operator and its row sum sit inside the identity, which explains both the deterministic bias and
its $\rhon$ amplification. A four-part theorem makes that structure explicit, and a controlled
$2\times2$ probe separates it from the cross-layer phenomenon addressed by probabilistic spiking
neurons: their transplant trails the closed-form correction by \soneTppGap{} pp at $T{=}4$,
while perturbing neighbour arrival phases inside one aggregation costs \soneIntra{} pp.
The natural inversion of a sufficient bound is falsified; we replace it with a calibrated
feasibility band that screens unreachable graphs correctly on all three held-out datasets.
The resulting correction is deterministic, requires no randomness, and plugs into standard
aggregations with a single hyperparameter.
