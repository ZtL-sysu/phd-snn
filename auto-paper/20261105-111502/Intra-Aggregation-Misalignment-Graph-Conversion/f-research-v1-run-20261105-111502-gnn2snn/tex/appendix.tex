\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:g1}}
\label{app:proof}

\paragraph{Symbol table.} $X$: node features; $\Ahat$: symmetric normalised adjacency with
self-loops; $\rhon=\max_i\sum_j\Ahat_{ij}$; $M_l,b_l$: layer-$l$ weight and bias;
$B_l=\Ahat H_{l-1}M_l+b_l$: ANN pre-activation; $H_l=\relu(B_l)$; $s_{l,t}\in\{0,1\}^{\hdim}$:
spike vector at step $t$; $c_l=\sum_t s_{l,t}$: emitted count;
$\Emat_l=c_l/T-\relu(B_l)$: rate error; $\theta$: threshold; $T$: timesteps;
$\beta$: forward bias; $u$: residual membrane; $\epsPP$: target error;
$G_L=\prod_l\|M_l\|_2$: cumulative spectral gain; $\mathrm{margin}$: median absolute logit gap.

\paragraph{(a) Exact identity.} The instantaneous input to layer $l\ge 2$ at step $t$ is
$\mathrm{inst}_{l,t}=\Ahat(s_{l-1,t}M_l)+b_l$. Summing over $t=1,\dots,T$ and dividing by $T$,
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{T}\sum_t \mathrm{inst}_{l,t}
&= \Ahat\!\left(\frac{c_{l-1}}{T}M_l\right)+b_l \notag\\
&= \Ahat\!\left[(\relu(B_{l-1})+\Emat_{l-1})M_l\right]+b_l \notag\\
&= B_l + \Ahat(\Emat_{l-1}M_l).
\end{align}
Identifying the left-hand side with the converted pre-activation $\hat{B}_l$ gives
$\hat{B}_l-B_l=\Ahat(\Emat_{l-1}M_l)$. For $l=1$ the input is the constant current
$\mathrm{inst}_{1}=\Ahat(XM_1)+b_1+\beta$, which contains no spike-dependent term, hence
$\Emat_0=0$ and $\hat{B}_1-B_1=\beta$. With $\beta=\betastar=\theta/(2T)$ the first-layer rate
error is a centred quantisation residual. The equality is exact; no expectation, independence or
activation-distribution assumption is used. This is the graph counterpart of the expectation
identity of \cite{tpp2025}, with $\Ahat$ entering as a multiplicative operator.

\paragraph{(b) Membrane identity.} The output unit accumulates without reset, so after $T$ steps
$\mathrm{mem}=\sum_{t=1}^T \mathrm{inst}_t = c_{\mathrm{out}}\theta+u$, where $c_{\mathrm{out}}$
is the number of threshold crossings and $u\in[0,\theta)$ the residual. The sum contains no
index $t$ inside its terms, hence it is invariant under any permutation $\pi$ of
$\{1,\dots,T\}$:
$\sum_t \mathrm{inst}_{\pi(t)}=\sum_t\mathrm{inst}_t$. A count-based readout is not invariant once
a reset is applied at an intermediate layer, because the reset couples the emission time to the
running membrane; this is exactly why the $L=3$ closed form fails.

\paragraph{(c) Reachability.} For a constant current $B+\beta$, IF emission satisfies
$c=\lfloor T(B+\beta)/\theta\rfloor$. If $B+\beta=k\theta/T$ for integer $k$, then
$c/T=(B+\beta)$ exactly and the converted activation is bit-exact. Otherwise
$c/T\in[B+\beta-\theta/T,\,B+\beta]$ and the residual lies on one of two adjacent grid points.
With $\beta=\theta/(2T)$ the quantisation residual is centred and uniformly bounded by
$\theta/(2T)$.

\paragraph{(d) Propagation bound.} From (a) and sub-multiplicativity,
$\|\Emat_l\|\le \|\Ahat\|\,\|M_l\|_2\,\|\Emat_{l-1}\|$. Unrolling,
$\|\Emat_L\|\le \rhon\, G_L\,\|\Emat_1\|$, and by (c)
$\|\Emat_1\|\le \theta\sqrt{\hdim}/(2T)$ when the centred bias is applied. Without it the
systematic component is $|\beta-\theta/(2T)|$ amplified by $\rhon$ at every hop, which we write
as the factor $(1+\gamma_\beta)$ with $\gamma_\beta$ linear in $\rhon$. Rearranging
$\|\Emat_L\|\le\epsPP$ gives the stated $T_L$. The bound's multiplier is the neighbourhood
geometry $\rhon$ and the cumulative gain $G_L$, not a per-layer weight norm alone; reducing
$\|W\|_\infty$ without changing $\rhon$ therefore cannot control graph conversion error.

\paragraph{Failure depth.} At $L\ge3$ an intermediate layer's \emph{count} depends on the
arrival order of its input once a reset is applied, so (c) no longer determines $\Emat_l$
for $l\ge2$. The bound (d) remains valid as a statement about the output membrane, but its
conservatism grows with depth. We report the empirical $L=3$ failure and the conservatism
table in the registry rather than extrapolating the two-layer closed form.

\section{Negative Controls}
\label{app:negative}
Spectral-norm-constrained training drives the weight gain towards one but shrinks activations,
so the absolute quantisation floor dominates. An adaptive threshold set from the $99.9$th
percentile of activation magnitude exceeds the unit ceiling by a factor of two to five and pushes
most units into saturation. Multi-view averaging over thresholds and initial potentials does not
beat the source ANN, while an ensemble of independently trained ANNs does. All three failures are
reported with their mechanisms and remain in the result registry.
