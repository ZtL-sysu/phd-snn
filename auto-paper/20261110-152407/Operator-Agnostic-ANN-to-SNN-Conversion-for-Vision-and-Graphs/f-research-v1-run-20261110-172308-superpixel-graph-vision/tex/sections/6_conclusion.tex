\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied whether a conversion framework assembled for graph node classification transfers to graph-structured visual classification, where images ship as superpixel graphs rather than regular grids, and whether it can deliver an ANN-relative energy advantage. A linear global pooling operator preserves the exact conversion-error identity $\Bh-B=R A(E_1W_2)$; a unified float64 forward pass verifies it to \IdentityMax. Within the transferred combination we found a genuine mechanistic redundancy: deterministic two-phase spiking already realises the target firing count, so the closed-form bias $\beta^\star=\theta/(2T)$ double-corrects the rate and must be removed. With that correction, membrane decoding, margin regularisation and a narrow low-timestep network, both 45\,nm arithmetic-energy conventions exceed $5\times$ across four mutually disjoint holdout groups and \TotalSeeds\ independent seeds, while all adapted single-point conversion baselines remain near chance.

The accompanying cost is an explicit accuracy--energy frontier: measured conversion loss is \CIFARLoss$\pm$\CIFARLossStd\,pp on CIFAR10 and \MNISTLoss$\pm$\MNISTLossStd\,pp on MNIST under the energy constraint, with modest teacher accuracies. We report the frontier and a negative scaling result rather than a simultaneous fidelity target. The broader lesson is that conversion is a statement about how a computation is discretised: on a graph the discretisation happens along the neighbourhood as well as along time, and a component that is correct for one operator can be wrong for another. Extending the analysis to dynamic or attention-based vision graphs, and replacing op-count estimates with hardware measurements, are the natural next steps.
