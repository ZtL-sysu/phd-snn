                                                Abstract
    Finite-precision message transport can change the final logits of a frozen graph neural network along
a conversion path. We study a narrow but auditable certificate for one exact frozen two-layer graph-
convolution chain. A rowwise uniform quantizer assigns each node a deterministic half-step error radius;
these radii are propagated through two normalized-adjacency operations, a non-expansive ReLU, and the
frozen downstream weight matrix. The resulting interval is compared with a valid norm upper bound
and a matched global-radius outputwise ablation. Across Cora, Chameleon and Squirrel, three frozen
checkpoint seeds, 4/8 quantization levels, and global/rowwise uniform quantizers, all 36 measured final-
logit errors are contained and all proposed bounds are analytically no looser than the norm comparator.
The 18 global-uniform cells provide an equality control. In the 18 rowwise cells, the proposed interval
reduces median bound width relative to the matched global-radius ablation by 74.26% to 82.12%, with
a standard median tightening of 79.65%. Absolute width relative to realized RMS error remains large,
with a median of 30.68 and a maximum of 36.91. The supported contribution is therefore deterministic
final-logit containment plus relative tightening over a matched radius-granularity ablation, not statistical
coverage, broad SNN superiority, robustness, or an absolute-width guarantee.

1    Introduction
Finite-precision conversion of a frozen graph neural network changes the numerical path from input features
to output logits. Quantized messages are aggregated by the graph operator, passed through nonlinearities,
aggregated again, and transformed by frozen downstream weights. A small elementwise message error can
therefore be amplified by graph connectivity and weight magnitude. Generic quantization metrics such as
bit count, message error, or average task accuracy do not tell a user whether the final logits remain inside a
computable worst-case envelope.
    Interval and norm bounds offer a natural certificate language, but their usefulness depends on preserving
the structure of the actual operator chain. A scalar global norm bound is easy to state but can merge all
nodes, hidden channels, and output classes into a single loose radius. Conversely, a bound estimated from
observed errors can become tautological if the same errors are used to construct the certificate. For frozen
conversion, a useful target is a deterministic bound whose radius comes from the quantizer definition, not
labels, logits, or realized residuals.
    This paper examines the exact chain

             M = XW0 ,        c = Qrow (M ),
                              M                    H = relu(AM + b0 ),      Z = AHW1 + bout ,
    and its quantized counterpart. Each row of M is quantized with a rowwise uniform lattice. The quantizer
step determines a half-step radius before any actual error is inspected. ReLU non-expansiveness and two
applications of the normalized adjacency operator yield a componentwise interval for the final logits.
    The main question is: can this rowwise deterministic interval contain every measured final-logit error
while materially tightening a matched global-radius ablation? We answer this question within a fixed frozen
two-layer GCN scope. The empirical contribution is relative tightening under deterministic containment.
We explicitly do not claim that the absolute worst-case width is small.
    The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 relates the study to graph-message quan-
tization, frozen ANN-to-SNN conversion, and interval certificates. Section 3 defines the exact computation,
quantizer, bounds and numerical policy. Section 4 reports the complete 36-cell matrix and its negative
width evidence. Section 5 separates analytic validity from empirical utility and states limitations. Section 6
concludes.

    Interval bound propagation and ReLU network verification supply the mathematical ingredients Gowal
et al. [2018], Wong and Kolter [2018], Mirman et al. [2018], Katz et al. [2017]. Componentwise ReLU is non-
expansive, and interval arithmetic propagates local coordinate radii through affine maps. The application-
specific step here is to retain the two graph-operator magnitudes and the rowwise message radius before
applying the frozen output weight. Nearby conversion-error work studies corrections and temporal/output
behavior Li et al. [2024], Huang et al. [2024]; it does not establish the deterministic finite-precision message
certificate evaluated here.
    The differentiation is therefore narrow: interval propagation itself is standard; the application-specific
contribution is retaining rowwise message radii through two graph operators and isolating their effect with a
matched global-radius ablation. We do not claim a new general interval theorem or broad SNN robustness.


3     Method
3.1    Frozen graph computation
Let X ∈ RN ×F be the node-feature matrix and A ∈ RN ×N the normalized adjacency matrix. The frozen
first-layer matrix and bias are W0 ∈ RF ×H and b0 ∈ RH . The hidden-width message is M = XW0 ∈ RN ×H .
The frozen output matrix and bias are W1 ∈ RH×C and bout ∈ RC . All nine frozen model snapshots have
H = 16; their tensor shapes are recorded in results/model-shapes.json and linked to model hashes in
the run provenance. The reference logits are

                                       Z = A relu(AM + b0 )W1 + bout .
    Stored graph and model snapshots are float32, but all quantized values, dequantized values, reconstruc-
tions and certificate arithmetic are evaluated in float64. This removes an unmodelled float32 reconstruction
term from the radius.

3.2    Rowwise uniform quantizer
For node i, let mi = minh Mih and ri = maxh Mih . For L ∈ {4, 8} levels, the step is

If si = 0, row i is copied exactly and receives zero radius. Endpoint clipping is only a safeguard; an
out-of-range source value beyond tolerance fails the cell. The deterministic radius matrix is

                                                  ∆ih = si /2.
    It is computed from the quantizer range before actual errors are inspected. No label, logits, realized
residual or held-out error is used to set ∆.

4.2     Deterministic containment and comparator validity
All 36 measured final-logit errors lie within Babs , and all 36 proposed bounds satisfy Babs ≤ Bmink under
the fixed tolerance. No cell contains NaN or infinity. The largest actual-minus-proposed excess is negative in
every row, so no observed error crosses the certificate. These findings validate the deterministic derivation
and its implementation; they are not statistical coverage estimates.

4.3     Radius-granularity ablation
The 18 global-uniform cells satisfy the expected equality control: Babs = Bglobal within tolerance, and
tightening is zero up to numerical precision. This result is important because it shows that the rowwise gain
is not produced by comparing different formulas under constant radius.
    In all 18 rowwise cells, the proposed interval is tighter than the matched global-radius ablation. Tighten-
ing ranges from 74.26% to 82.12%, with a standard median of 79.65%. The effect is consistent across graph,
checkpoint seed and 4/8-level conditions. Figure 2 shows per-cell width/RMS and tightening; Figure 3 uses
the same frozen rows to show the global equality control and rowwise tightening distribution.

4.4    Absolute width is a negative result
Worst-case interval width relative to realized RMS is large. For rowwise quantization, width/RMS has a
standard median of 30.68 and a maximum of 36.91. The global-uniform condition also ranges above ten
in several cells. This negative evidence rejects an absolute-width claim. The result is instead a relative
one: rowwise radii substantially tighten a matched global-radius envelope while retaining deterministic
containment.

4.5    Failure audit
The run contains zero clipping events and zero non-finite rows. Constant rows receive identity reconstruction
and zero radius. Every row records dataset, seed, level, quantizer and radius hash. The complete raw table is
retained; no failed or unfavorable cell is omitted. The main empirical improvement is the rowwise tightening
distribution, while absolute width remains an explicit limitation.

5     Discussion
The experiment separates three logically different statements. First, the interval derivation and implemen-
tation yield deterministic containment for the specified chain. Second, the inequality Babs ≤ Bmink follows
from norm ordering and serves as a validity check. Third, rowwise radius granularity produces an empirical
74.26% to 82.12% tightening over the matched global-radius ablation. Combining these statements into a
generic “certified SNN improvement” would overstate the evidence.
    The rowwise choice is appropriate for the tested message matrices because the quantizer range varies
substantially across nodes. It is not claimed to dominate per-channel, learned, vector, or codebook quantizers.

Those alternatives can reduce reconstruction error or adapt to data distributions, but they require different
radius and cost assumptions. The present study holds the lattice family fixed to isolate the certificate effect
of radius granularity.
    The high absolute width/RMS is not hidden. It arises because a worst-case independent-coordinate
interval allows all local errors to align adversarially, whereas realized quantization errors and graph paths
partially cancel. A smaller absolute certificate may require distributional assumptions, calibration with
independent data, correlation-aware bounds, or a multilayer abstraction. Such extensions would no longer
be the deterministic unconditional certificate studied here.
    The scope is limited to frozen two-layer hidden-16 GCN checkpoints on three static graphs and three
seeds. The work does not measure temporal spikes, hardware energy, adversarial robustness, uncertainty
calibration, learned quantizers, dynamic graphs, or multilayer nonlinear conversion. The global comparator
is a matched radius-granularity ablation rather than a broad state-of-the-art baseline. Accordingly, the
results support a narrow verification method and a controlled radius-granularity finding.


6    Conclusion
For the exact frozen two-layer chain studied in this paper, rowwise uniform message radii can be propagated
through two normalized-adjacency operations, ReLU and the frozen output weight to form a deterministic
final-logit interval. All 36 tested graph/seed/level/quantizer cells are contained, and the proposed interval is
analytically no looser than a valid norm comparator. Global-uniform quantization provides an exact equality
control. Rowwise radii tighten the matched global-radius ablation by 74.26% to 82.12%, with a median of
79.65%.
    The result does not establish small absolute certificate width: rowwise width/RMS has a median of 30.68
and a maximum of 36.91. The supported contribution is therefore deterministic final-logit containment plus
relative tightening over a matched global-radius ablation. Future work must address correlation-aware or
multilayer bounds before claiming smaller absolute error envelopes or broader GNN-to-SNN robustness.
