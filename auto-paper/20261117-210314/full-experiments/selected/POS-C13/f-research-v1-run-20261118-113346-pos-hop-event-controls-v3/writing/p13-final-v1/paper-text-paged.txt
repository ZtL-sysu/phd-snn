                                               Abstract
    Multi-hop positional messages preserve graph structure but are expensive to communicate in frozen
graph conversions. We study a narrow representation-coding problem on Chameleon and Squirrel: con-
catenate raw node features with one-, two- and three-hop normalized messages, quantize each channel
using eight Lloyd centroids plus one escape symbol, and account for four-bit symbols, 32-bit escape pay-
loads and per-channel codebooks. Across five split/probe seeds, hybrid reconstruction error is 3.952%
and 4.879%, while the conservative ledger reduces storage by 7.49× and 7.60×. Recomputation after
node permutation agrees to 2.22e-16. A codec-consistent shuffled-hop control, which quantizes globally
permuted hop blocks with unchanged ordered-channel codebooks, produces 3.72× and 3.63× mean error
ratios. Retrained hybrid probes lose 0.083 percentage points on Chameleon and gain 0.768 points on
Squirrel relative to raw features. Fixed raw-trained probes favor ordered over shuffled hybrid representa-
tions by 9.57 and 9.08 points. The contribution is a conversion-level multi-hop coding and order-control
protocol, not a general graph-transformer-to-SNN method.

1     Introduction
Graph positional information often appears as multi-hop propagation of features or structural identifiers.
Concatenating hop-wise messages increases feature width and communication cost, while simple global quan-
tization can distort channel distributions and rare values. Frozen conversion settings make the problem more
specific: the graph representation is fixed, and the goal is to serialize a positional message without changing
its node-permutation behavior.
    Compression error alone is not sufficient evidence. A useful study must also show whether the canonical
hop order matters, whether the representation is equivariant to node relabeling, and whether downstream
predictions survive without representation-specific retraining. These controls distinguish a structured hop
code from a generic high-dimensional quantizer.
    This paper evaluates the representation

                                          H = [X, AX, A2 X, A3 X],
   where A is a row-normalized graph operator. Each channel is encoded with eight Lloyd centroids and one
escape symbol. Escaped values are stored exactly as float32 payloads. The serialization cost is deliberately
conservative:

    where four bits encode one of eight centroid identifiers or the escape marker, f_escape is the escaped
fraction, and 256/N amortizes eight float32 centroids over N nodes.
    The central question is whether this hybrid code preserves ordered multi-hop messages at useful storage
reduction while remaining node-permutation equivariant and sensitive to hop order. We answer this within
two static graphs and explicitly separate three forms of evidence: representation fidelity/storage, retrained
linear-probe performance, and fixed raw-probe ordered/shuffled controls.


2     Related Work
Positional encodings and graph transformers represent structure through attention mechanisms, learnable
structural/positional representations or spectral attention Vaswani et al. [2017], Dwivedi et al. [2022], Kreuzer
and Hamilton [2021]. These methods motivate the importance of ordered structural channels, but they do
not provide the exact serialization ledger evaluated here.
    Graph-model compression studies degree-aware quantization, vector-quantized node representations, aggregation-
aware mixed precision and communication compression Tailor et al. [2021], Ding et al. [2021], Wang et al.
[2022], Zhu et al. [2023], Wan et al. [2023], Gholami et al. [2022], Jacob et al. [2018], Nagel et al. [2021]. Our
method uses standard Lloyd centroids and exact escapes; the contribution is their combined hop-message
accounting and order controls, not a new general quantizer.
    ANN-to-SNN conversion work addresses output codes, offsets, grouped neurons, early exit and trans-
former conversion Hwang and Kung [2025], Hao et al. [2023], Lv et al. [2024], Bu et al. [2022], Li et al.

    The differentiation is therefore narrow. The work combines multi-hop positional messages, a conservative
codebook/escape format, exact node-permutation recomputation and a non-tautological codebook-order
mismatch control for frozen graph representations. It does not claim a new positional encoding, a direct
graph-transformer conversion result or broad SNN superiority.


3     Method
3.1    Multi-hop representation
For node features X∈R^(N×F) and row-normalized adjacency A∈R^(N×N), the ordered representation is

                                      H = [X, AX, A2 X, A3 X] ∈ RN ×4F .
   The four blocks retain hop position. The task experiments use labeled nodes only; representation, bit
and permutation audits use all graph nodes.

3.2    Hybrid Lloyd/escape encoding
For each channel, Lloyd quantization estimates eight centroids. Every value receives one four-bit symbol:
one of eight centroid identifiers or an escape marker. Escaped values retain a 32-bit float payload. Each
channel stores eight float32 centroids. Here “event” means a source-side coded or escaped message symbol;
no temporal spike is generated.
    For representation rows, codebooks are fitted on all nodes and the largest reconstruction errors are
escaped using a threshold and every tied value, avoiding row-order-dependent top-k selection. For task rows,
codebooks and escape thresholds are fitted on training nodes only and reused unchanged on validation and
test nodes. Escapes are selected at the source encoder; labels and test logits are never used.
    The exact average ledger is

   Raw float32 uses 32 bits/value. The eight-centroid Lloyd baseline uses three-bit symbols and 256/N
codebook overhead. Uniform 8-level coding uses three-bit symbols plus two float32 range endpoints per
channel.

3.3    Permutation and shuffled-hop controls
For node permutation P, features, adjacency and labels are permuted consistently, H and the hybrid code
are recomputed, and the reconstruction is inverse-permuted before comparison. Deterministic sorted Lloyd
fitting and threshold-plus-ties escape selection make the audit invariant to row order.
     The shuffled-hop control uses one non-identity global permutation S of the four hop blocks. Ordered
codebooks are fitted on H; the shuffled raw target S H is quantized with the same codebooks and the same
escape-fraction policy. The error ratio is

seeds are aggregated as a graph-level mean; every individual row is retained.

3.4    Task probes
Two probe protocols are separated. The retrained protocol fits one linear probe per method, graph and split
seed. The fixed protocol fits one raw-feature probe and reuses its weights, normalization, split and test nodes
for raw, uniform, Lloyd, hybrid-ordered and hybrid-shuffled representations. Training uses split-train indices,
epoch selection uses validation, and final metrics use test indices only. results/task-provenance.json
hashes all ten label/split records, the task runner, probe source and exact train/selection/evaluation policy.

Raw and hybrid inverse-permutation errors are 2.22e-16, below the 1e-10 gate. The earlier top-k escape
implementation was row-order-sensitive; threshold-plus-all-ties selection removed that defect.
    All five shuffle seeds per graph are non-identity. Under the codec-consistent ordered-codebook control,
mean error ratios are 3.725× on Chameleon and 3.626× on Squirrel, with a minimum of 3.310×. Thus the
strong ratio gate passes without comparing different codec families.

4.4    Five-seed task results
For fixed raw probes, ordered hybrid balanced accuracy is 62.99±2.66% on Chameleon and 45.49±0.82%
on Squirrel. Shuffled hybrid means are 53.43±7.67% and 36.41±7.23%, giving ordered-minus-shuffled gaps
of 9.57 and 9.08 pp. These gaps are descriptive order-sensitivity evidence, not proof of causal positional
reasoning.

4.5    Leakage audit and negative evidence
The first task implementation fitted probes on all labels and produced implausibly high accuracy near 99%.
That run is preserved but excluded. The corrected implementation trains only on split-train indices, selects
checkpoints on validation and evaluates once on test. Representation values are unchanged, while task
accuracies fall to the accepted range above. No accepted row depends on the leaky run.


5     Discussion
The evidence supports three scoped statements. First, the conservative hybrid format preserves the tested
multi-hop representation with 3.95–4.88% error and 7.49–7.60× storage reduction. Second, node permuta-
tion is reproduced to machine precision after deterministic codebook and escape handling. Third, ordered
codebooks degrade under non-identity hop-block mismatch, and fixed raw probes prefer ordered over shuffled
hybrid features.
    The method does not establish that hop order causes a particular graph-transformer computation. Fixed
probes show compatibility with the raw ordering, while mismatched-codebook error measures channel-order
sensitivity. Retrained probes can compensate for some transformations, and their mixed accuracy differences
should not be promoted to general task superiority.
    The conservative ledger includes symbol, escape payload and centroid costs, but deployment metadata,
packet framing and runtime energy may add overhead. The study uses two heterophilic graphs and five
probe seeds. It does not include a complete graph-transformer model, temporal spikes, dynamic graphs,
adversarial perturbations or hardware measurements.
    The prior-art overlap is substantial because Lloyd quantization, exact escapes and permutation equiv-
ariance are established ingredients. The contribution is the combined frozen positional-message format and
its paired order controls. A stronger future claim would require real graph-transformer-to-SNN conversion,
broader graph families and energy-aware event timing.


6     Conclusion
Hybrid hop-event coding preserves ordered multi-hop graph messages at substantially lower conservative
storage in the tested frozen setting. All binding representation, storage, permutation and shuffled-hop gates
pass. Five-seed retrained task loss is bounded, and fixed raw probes show clear ordered-versus-shuffled gaps.
The result is a scoped conversion-level message-coding study, not a general graph-transformer-to-SNN or
temporal SNN claim.
